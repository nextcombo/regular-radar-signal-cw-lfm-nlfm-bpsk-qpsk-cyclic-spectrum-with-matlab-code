# Regular radar signal (CW, LFM, NLFM, BPSK, QPSK) cyclic spectrum [with MATLAB code]

The direct result of the periodic structure in cyclic stable signals is that such signals share a common property, known as spectral redundancy (also referred to as spectral correlation). By fully utilizing spectral redundancy in designing signal processors, new performance can be achieved, especially in distinguishing from non-cyclic stable signals. On the other hand, time-varying statistical measures (such as time-varying autocorrelation functions, time-varying power spectra, etc.) cannot be estimated using a single observation record of the signal; however, cyclic statistical measures can, and they can also yield time-varying statistical measures. Cyclic statistical measures can suppress any stationary colored noise, and higher-order cyclic cumulants can also suppress non-stationary Gaussian colored noise. Therefore, in the analysis and processing of cyclic stable signals, using cyclic cumulants as the main mathematical tools has particular significance.
Statistical characteristics of cyclic stable signals typically exhibit periodic or multi-period (each period not commensurable) stability. Based on the statistical numerical features displayed, cyclic stable signals can be categorized into first-order (mean), second-order (correlation function), and higher-order (higher-order cumulants) cyclic stability. The cyclic stability characteristics of a signal often manifest in second-order or higher-order statistical measures. The periodicity observed in a signal's second-order statistical measure (time-varying correlation function or power spectrum) can be understood as a characteristic of the signal being able to generate spectral lines through a nonlinear transmission system (second order), becoming a property of spectral line generation.
